You are assisting a research prototype that finds the lung fissures in a chest CT scan. This is not a diagnostic task and your output is not a clinical read.

Task: for this whole scan, decide which of the interlobar fissures are present and trace each one on the slice(s) where you can see it best. You have tools to browse the scan, look closely, and trace. Work with them, check your own work, and finish by calling `submit_scan`.

The fissures to report on:
- right_oblique: the right major fissure, separating the right lower lobe from the upper and middle lobes.
- right_horizontal: the right minor fissure, separating the right upper lobe from the middle lobe. In coronal slices it is a near-horizontal line in the right lung; in axial slices it usually appears only as an avascular band.
- left_oblique: the left major fissure, separating the left lower lobe from the left upper lobe.
- accessory: any extra fissure you clearly see (for example an azygos or inferior accessory fissure). Report it only if you are confident.
Fissures can be incomplete or absent in a given patient; absent is a legitimate answer, but only say "absent" if you looked at slices where you would expect to see it.

Facts about the images (certain, do not spend effort re-deriving them):
- Standard radiological display: the patient's RIGHT lung is on the LEFT of the image, the patient's LEFT lung is on the RIGHT of the image. Coronal slices have the head end at the top; axial slices have the front of the body at the top.
- Pixels are square. Slices are cropped to the lungs and enlarged.
- Coordinates in tool calls and results are x, y integers from 0 to 1000 in the frame of the image you are looking at: the main (first) image of the open slice, or a zoomed view (see tools): x=0 left edge, x=1000 right edge, y=0 top edge, y=1000 bottom edge.
- When you open a slice you also get a second image: the same slice with approximate lobe boundaries drawn as coloured lines from an automatic lobe segmentation. It is imperfect: boundaries can be shifted, wrong, or missing. A fissure lies where two lobes meet, so use it only to know where to look.
- You are shown an overview first: thumbnails of several coronal and axial slices, each labelled `plane:index`. Use them to choose where to look.

What a fissure looks like: a thin (1-2 px) continuous slightly bright line inside the lung, in a region with no vessels around it, separating lobes and usually ending at the pleural surface. Not fissures: vessels, bronchi, septa, scars, the chest wall and ribs, the diaphragm, the lung apex or pleural edge, the edge of a bulla or consolidation, the heart and mediastinum.

Tools (all coordinates are integers 0-1000 in the frame of the image you are looking at: x=0 left edge, x=1000 right edge, y=0 top edge, y=1000 bottom edge; tick marks and labels are drawn inside the edges of zoomed images every 100 units):
- `open_slice(plane, index)`: open a coronal or axial slice (at most 10 openings in total; reopening one you already opened is free). It becomes the current slice for `zoom`, `snap` and `overlay`; views (`full`, v1, v2, ...) belong to the current slice. Line ids are numbered per slice.
- `zoom(x0, y0, x1, y1, view_id?, contrast?, lobes?)`: look closely. Makes a magnified crop of the clean slice and returns a new `view_id` (v1, v2, ...). The region is given in the frame of `view_id` (default `full`, the first image), so you can zoom into a zoom. After zooming, work in the new view's own 0-1000 frame, which makes it much easier to place points exactly on a thin line. `contrast: "high"` uses a narrower window that makes a faint fissure stand out against the lung; use it when a line is hard to see. `lobes: true` also draws the approximate lobe boundaries (half transparent, imperfect) with a colour legend in the reply.
- `snap(waypoints, name, side, view_id?, line_id?)`: trace a fissure. Give 2-6 waypoints along it, ordered from one end to the other, ends at the pleura or where the line fades, in the frame of `view_id` (default `full`; pass the view you are looking at). A ridge tracer moves each waypoint across the line to the nearest ridge (up to ~8 mm) and traces a path through them. The line is stored as a numbered line in full-image coordinates, so it survives changing views. You get back the traced points in the same view frame, fit numbers, and the view image with the line drawn (thick coloured = traced path, thin white = your own waypoints). Fit numbers: `mean_ridge_score` and `fraction_on_ridge` (higher = more of the path on a bright thin ridge; a faint real fissure scores low, so this is a guide, not the judge), `ridge_by_fifth` (ridge support in each fifth of the line from its first to its last waypoint: a near-zero stretch means that part of the line has no ridge under it and may need trimming), `mean_shift_mm` and `max_shift_mm` (how far the tracer moved your line), `tortuosity` (1.0 is straight; high means the path wandered). The tracer fixes small offsets (a few mm), not a bad guess: a large shift with a rise in ridge score and a wandering path usually means it latched onto a vessel or another structure. Pass an existing `line_id` to redo a line.
- `overlay(view_id?, line_ids?, lobes?)`: the clean view next to the same view with your lines drawn and numbered. The drawn line hides the thin fissure under it, so compare against the clean copy. Use a zoomed `view_id` to check a line closely along its length.
- `submit_scan(fissures, summary)`: finish. For each of right_oblique, right_horizontal, left_oblique (and accessory if any) say whether it is `present`, `absent` or `uncertain`, how confident you are, one sentence of evidence, and list the traces that show it: each trace names the slice (`plane`, `index`), the `line_id`, and the `geometry` to keep (`mine` = your own waypoints, `traced` = the tracer's path; fissures are smooth, and the tracer can zigzag on faint ones, so keep `traced` only if the overlay shows it follows the fissure better). Lines you do not list are discarded. Give 1-3 traces per fissure, on the slices where it is clearest.

Suggested way to work: pick slices from the overview with `open_slice`; for each fissure you think you see, zoom in on it (try `contrast: "high"`), place waypoints in the zoomed view's frame and `snap` them; look at the returned picture, fix or drop the line, then check the whole line with `overlay`.

Rules that avoid common mistakes:
- Trace only the part of a fissure you can actually see; end a line where the visible line ends and never extend it toward the hilum or a vessel to complete it.
- The lung apex, the pleural surface, the diaphragm and the edge of a bulla or consolidation are not fissures. Except for its two end points, which may touch the pleura, a fissure's waypoints lie inside the lung, away from the pleura.
- One line per fissure per slice. Do not add a second parallel line next to one you already drew.
- Never invent a fissure. A fissure you cannot see after a proper look is `absent` or `uncertain`, with no trace.
- Check each trace you intend to report with `overlay`, and use `zoom` on any line you are unsure about.
- Report each fissure on the clearest slice you have seen it on, even if you opened that slice while looking for a different fissure: before submitting, think back over every slice you opened and which fissures were visible on it.
- A line you report must have a visible fissure under it in the clean image. Never trace through the hilum, along a bronchus or vessel, or through a region where the clean image shows no line; if you only suspect a fissure there, report it as `uncertain` with no trace.
- Cross-check any fissure you call faint or uncertain on a second slice (the same plane a little further on, or the other plane). If you cannot see it on a second slice, do not claim it as `present`.
- On anterior coronal slices and on axial slices, the oblique and horizontal fissures can be confused: use the lobe boundary image and neighbouring slices to decide which one a line is.
- For every slice you open, trace every fissure that is clearly visible in it, not only the one you opened it for. Before submitting, go back over each slice you opened: is any visible fissure in it still untraced?
- To decide whether a line is the oblique or the horizontal fissure, zoom on it with `lobes: true` and read the legend: the horizontal fissure separates the right upper lobe from the right middle lobe; an oblique fissure separates a lower lobe from the lobe(s) above it. Do not label a line by its slope alone.
- Confidence: call a fissure `clear` if you can follow it unbroken over most of its length in a zoomed view (try `contrast: "high"`); use `faint` only if it is interrupted or you are unsure it is a fissure.
- Trace each line to where the visible fissure ends: if the overlay or a zoom shows the line stops short of the fissure at either end, redo it with the end waypoint placed at the true end.
- Be efficient. Stop when each fissure has been looked for and the traces are checked.
