What a fissure looks like: a thin, continuous, slightly bright line (about 1-2 px wide) inside the lung, in a region with no vessels around it. It separates lobes and usually ends at the pleural surface at both ends. Not fissures: vessels, bronchi, septa, scars, the chest wall and ribs, the diaphragm, the lung apex or pleural edge, the edge of a bulla or consolidation, the heart and mediastinum.

Which fissures can appear (a guide only; report what you see, not what is expected):
- Right lung: oblique (major) and horizontal (minor). Left lung: oblique (major) only. In a coronal slice the left lung has no horizontal fissure.
- Coronal: the horizontal fissure is a near-horizontal line in the right lung; the oblique fissures are seen obliquely and are often hazy.
- Axial: the oblique fissure is a diagonal line from the mediastinal side outward and posteriorly, but near the hilum it can look nearly horizontal or curved; follow the slope you see. The horizontal fissure appears as an avascular zone, rarely a crisp line.

Tools. Every tool except `submit` requires `slice`, the slice number it acts on; there is no default, so always say which slice you mean. A slice number outside {lo}-{hi} is rejected with the valid range. Views (`full`, v1, v2, ...) are regions of the common frame, so a view you made on one slice can be used on any other slice. Line ids are unique across slices; `mark` with an existing `line_id` redoes that line on its own slice. Every tool can also return the completed image with `completed`: `with_lines` = the slice (in the view you called the tool with) as it would be submitted, with every line currently traced on it; `clean` = the full slice without lines; omit it for no extra image. Use it to compare slices or to check what a slice looks like with all its lines before you submit. (all coordinates are integers 0-1000 in the frame of the image you are looking at: x=0 left edge, x=1000 right edge, y=0 top edge, y=1000 bottom edge; tick marks and labels are drawn inside the edges of zoomed images every 100 units):
- `look(slice, view_id?, lobes?, completed?)`: view slice `slice` clean, with tick marks, in the view `view_id` (default `full`, the whole slice). To scroll to a neighbouring slice at the same zoom you were working at, pass the same `view_id`: the view is the same region on every slice. `lobes: true` also draws the approximate lobe boundaries with a colour legend.
- `zoom(slice, x0, y0, x1, y1, view_id?, contrast?, lobes?, completed?)`: look closely. Makes a magnified crop of the clean slice and returns a new `view_id` (v1, v2, ...). The region is given in the frame of `view_id` (default `full`, the first image), so you can zoom into a zoom. After zooming, work in the new view's own 0-1000 frame, which makes it much easier to place points exactly on a thin line. `contrast: "high"` uses a narrower window that makes a faint fissure stand out against the lung; use it when a line is hard to see. `lobes: true` also draws the approximate lobe boundaries (half transparent, imperfect) with a colour legend in the reply.
- `check_points(slice, waypoints, view_id?, connect?, completed?)`: preview where your waypoints land before you commit them. It shows the clean view next to the same view with your points as numbered rings, and stores nothing. Place the points in the frame of `view_id`; the thin line you mean to follow should pass through the centre of every ring. If a point is off the line, correct its coordinates and check again; repeat until every ring is on the line, then call `mark` with the corrected points. It is free to use as often as you like.
- `mark(slice, waypoints, name, side, view_id?, line_id?, completed?)`: draw a fissure line. Give waypoints along the fissure, ordered from one end to the other, ends at the pleura or where the line fades, in the frame of `view_id` (default `full`; pass the view you are looking at). The line is your waypoints joined by straight segments, so use as many points as it takes to follow the curve: add a point wherever the fissure bends. The line is stored as a numbered line in full-image coordinates, so it survives changing views. You get back the view image with the line drawn and a circle at each of your points: check that every point sits ON the visible line, and if a point is off, redo the line with corrected points by passing the same `line_id`. Keep refining until each point is on the line.
- `overlay(slice, view_id?, line_ids?, lobes?, completed?)`: the clean view next to the same view with your lines drawn and numbered. The drawn line hides the thin fissure under it, so compare against the clean copy. Use a zoomed `view_id` to check a line closely along its length.
- `submit(lines)`: finish. Lines can be on any slice you traced; the lines on slice {start} are what is delivered, and lines on other slices are kept as extra. For each line give its `line_id`, a `status` (confirmed = on a visible fissure, uncertain = you are unsure it is a fissure or that it is placed right, dropped = not a fissure), and one sentence of evidence. Lines you leave out are dropped.

Suggested way to work: look at the full image; scroll a few neighbouring slices if a line is ambiguous; for each fissure you think you see, zoom in on it (try `contrast: "high"`), place waypoints in the zoomed view's frame, `check_points` them until every ring is on the line, then `mark` them; look at the returned picture, correct any point that is off the line by redoing the line, then check the whole line with `overlay` at a zoom level where you can see its full length.

Working order:
- Look at the result of `check_points` before you call the tool that draws the line; do not issue them together.
- Work on ONE fissure at a time. Finish it completely (zoom on it, place and check its points until every ring is on the line, draw it, verify it with an overlay) before you start looking for the next fissure. Keep a short list of the fissures you still have to look for, and take them one by one.

Rules that avoid common mistakes:
- Trace only the part of a fissure you can actually see. End a line where the visible line ends; never extend it toward the hilum or a vessel to complete it.
- Finish every line. Run each line all the way to where the visible fissure ends: to the pleural surface if the fissure reaches it, or to the point where it fades out. Do not stop early. The two end points are the part most often left unfinished, so before you submit, look at both ends of every line in a zoomed view; if the visible fissure continues past the last point, add points along it until it really ends, and check the new points with `check_points`. You can also scroll (`look` with the same `view_id`) to a neighbouring slice to see where the fissure ends.
- Do not go past the visible line either: each end point must sit on a visible part of the line, never on a vessel or on texture where you only expect the fissure to continue. Do not extend a line along a vessel toward the hilum.
- Do not interpolate. Never draw a line across a stretch where you cannot see the fissure, and do not bridge a gap between two visible stretches. If the fissure disappears and reappears, draw each visible stretch as its own separate line. Every point you place must have a visible line under it, not an expected one.
- The lung apex, the pleural surface, the diaphragm and the edge of a bulla or consolidation are not fissures. Except for its two end points, which may touch the pleura, a fissure's waypoints lie inside the lung, away from the pleura.
- One line per fissure. Do not add a second parallel line next to one you already have.
- Never invent a fissure to fill the expected set. Submitting no lines is a correct answer if you see none.
- Place each point on the thin bright line itself, not beside it; judge every point in a zoomed view.
- Use zoom to check a line you are unsure of, after tracing it, not only before.
- Be efficient: a typical slice needs a few tool calls. Stop when your lines are checked.
