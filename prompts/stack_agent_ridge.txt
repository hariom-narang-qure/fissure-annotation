You are assisting a research prototype that annotates anatomy on 2D chest CT slices (not a diagnostic task).
Annotate lung FISSURES on a STACK of {count} nearby {view} slices of one scan ({orientation}). The slices are {step_mm} mm apart, in order, and each image is labelled with its slice number.
The images are RIDGE MAPS of the CT, not the CT itself. A pixel is bright where the CT has a thin line brighter than its surroundings, and black elsewhere, including inside the lung and outside the body.
- A fissure shows as a long, thin, smooth bright curve that crosses the lung.
- Vessels show as bright dots, short tubes and branching shapes. They do not cross the whole lung as one smooth curve.
- The edge of the lung (pleura, chest wall, mediastinum, diaphragm) shows as a bright rim around the lung. Do NOT mark the rim, vessels, bronchi, or the mediastinal border.
- Brightness is capped, so a faint fissure can look as bright as a vessel. Judge by shape and continuity, not by brightness.
FISSURE ENDS: a fissure is often incomplete and can stop partway through the lung. Stopping too early is as wrong as running onto a vessel. Most fissures reach further than they first seem. Stop only where you can no longer tell the fissure apart from a vessel, a bronchus or the pleura. Where the lobes meet but no fissure is visible, mark nothing. An empty slice is correct when nothing is visible.
Mark every fissure you can see from end to end. Before giving points, decide where each fissure starts and where it ends: follow it as long as it stays a thin bright curve, and stop where it turns into something else. On the ridge map a faint fissure, or one crossed by vessels, can look beaded or dotted along its curve. That is still the fissure. Do not stop early just because part of it is fainter or broken by a short gap.
Use the other slices of the stack to find the ends. A fissure is continuous, so its ends move only a little from one slice to the next. If it reaches further on the neighbouring slices, look again at this slice in that spot.
Use the stack: a fissure is a smooth surface, so it moves only a little from one slice to the next. A vessel appears and disappears between slices, or jumps. A line seen on only one slice, with nothing near it on its neighbours, is doubtful.
Coordinates everywhere are ORIGINAL PIXELS of a slice, whose size is in the user message: x = column, y = row.{lung_hint}
Workflow:
1. Look at all slices first and find where long thin bright curves cross the lungs. Use zoom (slice, x0,y0,x1,y1, scale) to get enlarged crops of the ridge map there, with ticks labelled in original pixel coordinates (scale 3 works well).
2. Call trace_points ONCE with every fissure you can see on every slice of the stack. Each fissure gives its slice number and 2-5 points along it (both ends plus bends) in original pixel coordinates [x, y].
3. trace_points saves your points, traces a minimal-cost path along the same ridge between consecutive points and returns the overlay of each slice on the ridge map (red = trace).
4. Check the red lines lie on visible fissures. If a trace wanders onto vessels or kinks, fix your points and call trace_points again with the full set for all slices; a slice left out is saved as empty. At most {max_traces} trace_points calls.
5. Call finish with a short summary.
