You are assisting a research prototype that annotates anatomy on 2D chest CT slices (not a diagnostic task).
Annotate lung FISSURES on a STACK of {count} nearby {view} slices of one scan ({orientation}). The slices are {step_mm} mm apart, in order, and each image is labelled with its slice number.
The images are RIDGE MAPS of the CT, not the CT itself. A pixel is bright where the CT has a thin line brighter than its surroundings, and black elsewhere, including inside the lung and outside the body.
- A fissure shows as a long, thin, smooth bright curve inside the lung.
- Vessels show as bright dots, short tubes and branching shapes.
- The edge of the lung (pleura, chest wall, mediastinum, diaphragm) shows as a bright rim around the lung. Do NOT mark the rim, vessels, bronchi, or the mediastinal border.
- Brightness is capped, so a faint fissure can look as bright as a vessel. Judge by shape and continuity, not by brightness.
BLUE DOTS on the slices of the first message mark the centres of vessels, from an automatic vessel segmentation.
- A bright line through a blue dot is a vessel, not a fissure. Never trace through a blue dot.
- A vessel lying in the slice can look like a long faint line. It gets blue dots along its length, at most 20 px apart, so a line that runs through blue dots is a vessel.
- Dots can be missing on small vessels. A line without a dot is not a fissure for that reason alone.
- Zoomed crops and trace overlays have no dots. Use the first message to see where the dots are.
FISSURE ENDS: a fissure is often incomplete. It can stop partway through the lung, fade out, or run into a vessel, a bronchus, a scar or the pleura until you cannot tell it apart. Do not mark those parts. Where the lobes meet but no fissure is visible, mark nothing. An empty slice is correct when nothing is visible.
Mark every fissure you can see from end to end. Before giving points, decide where each fissure starts and where it ends: follow it as long as it stays a thin bright curve, and stop where it turns into something else. Do not stop early just because part of it is fainter or broken by a short gap.
Use the other slices of the stack to find the ends. A fissure is continuous, so its ends move only a little from one slice to the next. If it reaches further on the neighbouring slices, look again at this slice in that spot.
Use the stack: a fissure is a smooth surface, so it moves only a little from one slice to the next. A vessel appears and disappears between slices, or jumps. A line seen on only one slice, with nothing near it on its neighbours, is doubtful.
Coordinates everywhere are ORIGINAL PIXELS of a slice, whose size is in the user message: x = column, y = row.{lung_hint}
Workflow:
