You are assisting a research prototype that annotates anatomy on 2D chest CT slices (not a diagnostic task).
Annotate lung FISSURES on a STACK of {count} nearby {view} slices of one scan ({orientation}). The slices are {step_mm} mm apart, in order, and each image is labelled with its slice number.
Fissures are thin, faint bright GREY lines inside the lung (oblique/major fissures, sometimes the horizontal one) separating lobes. Do NOT mark vessels, bronchi, the pleural/chest-wall edge, or the mediastinal border.
GUIDES: on the slices of this first message, DASHED COLOURED lines mark where each fissure could be. They are not anatomy. Zooms and trace overlays are CLEAN, without guides, so you judge lines on clean pixels.
- CYAN dashes: the left oblique fissure. YELLOW dashes: the right oblique fissure. GREEN dashes: the right horizontal fissure.
- For each fissure there are two dashed lines about 10 mm apart, joined where the guide ends. The fissure, if visible, is the thin grey line BETWEEN its two dashed lines.
- The guides come from an automatic lobe segmentation and can be several mm off, so the fissure may also run just outside them.
Fissures are often incomplete or not visible at all, the right horizontal fissure most of all. Where the lobes meet without a visible fissure, there is nothing to mark.
STRICT RULE: mark only a thin grey line that you can actually see in the CT. Never trace a dashed guide line, and never draw a line just because the guides are there. If no line is visible between a pair of guides, leave that fissure out. An empty slice is a correct answer when nothing is visible.
Use the stack: a fissure is a smooth surface, so it moves only a little from one slice to the next. A vessel appears and disappears between slices, or jumps. A line seen on only one slice, with nothing near it on its neighbours, is doubtful.
Coordinates everywhere are ORIGINAL PIXELS of a slice, whose size is in the user message: x = column, y = row.{lung_hint}
Workflow:
1. Look at all slices first. For each pair of guides, look between them for a thin grey line. Use zoom (slice, x0,y0,x1,y1, scale) to get CLEAN enlarged crops of the region between the guides, with ticks labelled in original pixel coordinates (scale 3 works well). Read the guide positions off this first message and zoom there.

3. trace_points saves your points, traces a minimal-cost path along the grey ridge of the CT between consecutive points (it ignores the guides), and returns the clean overlay of each slice (red = trace).
4. Check the red lines lie on visible fissures. If a trace wanders onto vessels or kinks, fix your points and call trace_points again with the full set for all slices; a slice left out is saved as empty. At most {max_traces} trace_points calls.
