1. Ends. The first and last point of a line must each sit on a visible part of the fissure. A line that stops well short of where the fissure is still visible, runs on past where it fades, or ends at the chest wall or pleura without the fissure reaching there, breaks this rule. A line may legitimately end where the fissure reaches the pleura or fades out near the hilum.
2. No interpolation. Every part of a line must have a visible fissure under it. A stretch drawn across an area where no fissure can be seen, or a line bridging the gap between two visible stretches, breaks this rule. Where a fissure disappears and reappears, each visible stretch should be its own line.
3. Points on the line. Every numbered point must sit on the thin bright line, not beside it. A point that is clearly off the visible line, or a line that is offset from the fissure along its length, breaks this rule.
4. Smooth path. A fissure is smooth and gently curved. Zigzags and kinks are wrong, and so are straight segments that cut across a curve (the points are too sparse there).
5. No duplicates. Two lines on the same fissure, or a second parallel line beside a real one, break this rule.
6. Right structure. A line must follow a fissure, not a vessel, bronchus, septum, scar, rib or chest wall, the diaphragm, the lung apex or pleural edge, the edge of a bulla or consolidation, or the heart or mediastinum.
7. The name fits the anatomy, not the picture. The right lung has an oblique and a horizontal (minor) fissure; the left lung normally has only an oblique one (a rare left minor fissure exists, so report such a line as uncertain, not wrong). How a line looks in the picture is NOT evidence for its name: on axial slices the left oblique fissure often runs nearly horizontally across the image, which is normal and is not a problem. Flag a name only when it clearly contradicts the anatomy.
8. Missed fissures. If a clearly visible fissure in the slice has no line, report it, saying where it is in words (no coordinates).
